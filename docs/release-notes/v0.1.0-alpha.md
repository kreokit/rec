**Free, offline screen recording.** This is the first public alpha of KreoKit Rec: record your screen, a region or a window, with your camera and microphone (and the computer's sound where your system allows it), then trim, place your camera bubble, clean up your voice and export an MP4. Everything runs on your own computer. No account, no upload, no watermark.

> ⚠️ **Alpha.** Expect rough edges, and please read "Tested so far" below: Rec has been measured on one Mac only. If anything goes wrong, please tell us: ⋯ → **Report a problem** in the app, or https://github.com/kreokit/rec/issues.

## What it does

- **Record** the whole screen, a region you drag, or a single window, at 1080p or 720p, 30 frames a second. Camera, microphone and the computer's sound are each recorded as their own track, so a crash or power cut keeps everything up to the last second or two, and Rec offers to recover it.
- **While recording:** a 3-2-1 countdown, a small floating control (time, level, pause, stop) and a bubble that shows your camera. Rec keeps its own windows out of the video where the system allows it; elsewhere the controls move to the menu bar (macOS) or tray (Windows). Stop from any app with **⌥⇧⌘S** (macOS) or **Ctrl+Alt+Shift+S** (Windows, Linux X11).
- **Never lose a recording:** warnings when disk space runs low, and the recording stops and finishes by itself before the disk is full. Picture and sound are checked after every recording.
- **Finish:** trim the start and end, choose screen only, screen with your camera in a bubble (circle or rounded, any corner, three sizes) or camera only, clean up your voice (RNNoise, built in) and set the loudness (Social −14 LUFS, Tutorial & podcast −16 LUFS, or keep).
- **Export** an MP4 (H.264 + AAC), checked after writing; nothing is overwritten without asking.

## Tested so far

- **Measured on one computer:** an Intel MacBook Pro with macOS 12. Fresh frames, picture-sound sync, crash recovery, region, window, camera, countdown, the floating control and bubble, the stop shortcut and the disk-space stop were measured there with the real screen, camera and microphone.
- **Windows and Linux** run the whole recorder in automated tests with made-up sources, and record their real (virtual) screen on the build machines.
- **Only informally tested** (friends tried the test version and said it works; nothing was measured):
  - the **computer's sound on macOS 13 and later**, and recording a region or a window there;
  - **cameras and microphones on Windows and Linux**;
  - **Linux screen sharing** (the system's screen-sharing dialog on Wayland, GNOME and KDE);
  - keeping Rec's own windows out of the video on macOS 13+ and Windows, and the tray on Windows.

If one of these doesn't work for you, ⋯ → **Report a problem** helps us most: it says which system you're on.

## Installing an unsigned app

The alpha isn't signed with paid developer certificates, so your system will warn you the first time.

**macOS**
1. Open the `.dmg` and drag **KreoKit Rec** to Applications.
2. Open it. If macOS says it "can't be opened" or "can't verify the developer", open **System Settings → Privacy & Security** (on macOS 12: **System Preferences → Security & Privacy → General**), and click **Open Anyway**, then confirm.
3. If macOS instead says the app "is damaged and can't be opened", that is the same missing signature, not real damage. Run this once in Terminal, then open the app again:
   ```bash
   xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine "/Applications/KreoKit Rec.app"
   ```

**Windows**
1. Run the `…-setup.exe` installer.
2. If SmartScreen says "Windows protected your PC", click **More info → Run anyway**.

**Linux**
- AppImage: `chmod +x KreoKit.Rec_*.AppImage`, then run it.
- Debian / Ubuntu: `sudo apt install ./KreoKit.Rec_*_amd64.deb`.

## Known limitations

- 30 frames a second only: 60 didn't keep enough fresh frames on the Mac it was measured on.
- The computer's sound needs macOS 13 or later (on macOS 12 Rec records screen, camera and microphone).
- Linux: no tray icon (the library it needs would bring GPL code into the app), no region with the screen-sharing dialog, no stop shortcut on Wayland, and cameras that only send MJPEG aren't supported yet. Rec's own windows can't be kept out of a Linux recording: its window steps aside to the taskbar.
- Cutting in the middle of a recording, zooming in on clicks and captions aren't there yet.
